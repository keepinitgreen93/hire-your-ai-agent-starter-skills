# Texting and compliance walkthrough

Use Product Champ words with the owner. The official source pages for every step are in the internal sources file. Re-read them before you start, because screens change. Walk one step at a time and wait for the owner after each one.

Say this once at the start: "Phone carriers decide whether a texting registration is approved. I can help you get ready. I cannot promise approval, and this is not legal advice."

Nothing in this file is submitted by the agent. The owner presses every submit button. By default nothing is submitted during a trial.

## Part 1: Is the business ready to register for texting

Check each line. Mark READY, NEEDS OWNER or CAN'T TELL.

1. The legal business name is known and matches the tax paperwork exactly. The owner types the tax ID themselves. Never ask for it in chat and never save it.
2. The business name, address and phone are the same in the business profile, on the website and in the registration details. Use the audit result. A mismatch must be fixed first.
3. The website is live, public and clearly about this business.
4. The website has a Privacy Policy page and a Terms page, linked in the footer. The Privacy Policy says mobile information is not shared with third parties or affiliates for marketing. The Terms describe the text program, how often messages come, that message and data rates may apply, and how to stop and get help.
5. The business email is on the business's own domain, not a free mail address.
6. There is a local number in the account that can text.
7. The owner knows which kind of texts they send: marketing, non-marketing such as reminders and updates, or both.
8. No business with a tax ID uses the path meant for people without one.

Then the owner can look at the current state on screen: **Settings, Phone System, Trust Center**. Ask what the texting card shows: not started, pending, rejected or approved. The connection cannot read this, so the owner's answer is the source.

- Pending: wait. Do not file a second one.
- Rejected: open View required fixes for every reason. Fix all of them before sending it again.
- Approved: open **Settings, Phone System, Phone Numbers** and look for the green verified label under the number. If it is missing, link the number to the approved campaign.

## Part 2: The chat widget and consent

There are two different things here. Explain which one the owner is looking at.

**A. An ordinary chat widget.** Open **Sites, Chat Widget**, open the widget, then **Chat window, Additional options, Compliance notice and consent**. Check with the owner:

1. The legal notice is shown.
2. The consent checkbox is on.
3. "Make this checkbox required" is off. Agreeing to texts must be a choice.
4. "Pre-check the box by default" is off. The visitor must tick it themselves.
5. The consent wording names the business, the kind of messages, that frequency varies, that message and data rates may apply, and how to stop and get help.
6. The widget or the page footer links to the Privacy Policy and the Terms.
7. Marketing texts and non-marketing texts are separate choices when the business sends both.

Consent given through the widget is saved on the contact's activity. Any workflow that texts people who came in through chat should text only people who ticked the box. If the audit could read the web chat workflow, report whether it checks consent. If it could not, mark it CAN'T TELL and ask the owner to open the workflow.

**B. The chat widget made during texting registration.** When the owner registers a campaign, the registration opens on a chat widget setup by default. Product Champ writes the consent wording, the stop and help wording and the notices, and locks them. Only colors and the header can be changed. The steps, for the owner to do when their plan allows:

1. Open **Settings, Phone System, Trust Center** and continue to campaign registration.
2. Stay on the chat widget setup, unless the business already collects consent another approved way. Then switch to manual setup.
3. Choose the message type: marketing, informational, or mixed. Mixed creates two separate consent boxes.
4. Answer the questions about the content. Age limited or financial content adds a required date of birth field.
5. Review the widget. Do not expect to edit the consent text.
6. Put the widget code on the website page named in the registration. It must be live and public before the review.
7. Make sure that page has no other form that asks for texting consent.
8. Run the built in review, fix what it lists, then submit. Passing that review is not carrier approval.
9. Watch the status in the Trust Center. Do not create a duplicate.

Not confirmed: no separate automation called a "chat widget compliance workflow" was found in the official documentation. If the owner asks for one, say so plainly and offer the two checks above.

## Part 3: The Trust Center

The Trust Center is the screen under **Settings, Phone System** where a business registers for texting and for trusted calling. It shows the brand, the campaign, their status and any required fixes. It can also hold caller name and call verification items for voice calls.

There is also a provider-level view that only the company providing the account can open. A member cannot see it. If the owner cannot find a setting, or needs a registration checked from the provider side, the next step is to ask their Product Champ support contact. Do not send the owner looking for a provider screen.

## What to do at the end

1. Give each line in Part 1 and Part 2A a status in the report.
2. List the exact fixes, in order.
3. Offer the `texting-ready-kit` skill for the Privacy Policy, Terms and opt-in wording.
4. Remind the owner: submit only when the plan is active and they accept the cost shown on screen and the chance of rejection.
