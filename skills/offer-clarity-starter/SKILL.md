# Offer Clarity Starter

This skill is step 1 of the Day 3 chain in the Hire Your AI Agent challenge from Truly Authentic Marketing (TAM). It interviews the owner and writes down ONE offer in plain words: who it is for, the result, what is included, the price or next step, and a real reason to act now. A quiz, an ad, or a video cannot match an offer that is still fuzzy. So we fix the offer first.

It drafts. It does not publish, send, or change any account.

## When to Use

- The owner is on Day 3 and is about to plan a quiz.
- The owner says "I sell a few things" or "I am not sure what to promote."
- The owner wants an ad, a quiz, and a video that all say the same thing.

Do not use it to invent a new business. It sharpens what the owner already sells.

## Prerequisites

- The starter Company Brain from `company-brain-starter`. Read it first.
- The customer profile from `dream-customer-avatar-starter`. Read it too.
- If either is missing, tell the owner. Offer to run that skill first, or ask the owner to paste the facts. Do not guess.

## Procedure

1. Read the Company Brain and the customer profile. Say back, in two lines, what you think the owner sells and who it is for. Ask if that is right. Done when the owner says yes or corrects you.
2. Ask which ONE thing the owner wants more people to say yes to in the next 90 days. If there are many, help the owner choose one. Done when one offer is named.
3. Interview the owner one question at a time. Wait for each answer. If an answer is thin, ask one follow up. Cover:
   - Who is this for? Who is it NOT for?
   - What result do they want? What does life look like when they get it?
   - What do they get? List what is included, in plain words.
   - How long does it take, and how is it delivered (call, class, visit, kit, event)?
   - What does it cost, or what is the next step (book a call, free visit, quote)?
   - What real reason is there to act now? Examples: a date, a limited number of spots, a season. If there is no true reason, say so. Do not invent one.
   - What real proof exists? Reviews, photos, numbers the owner can show.
   - What must the offer never promise? (This protects the owner.)
4. Write the One Offer Sheet (format below). Show it as a preview. Done when the owner has read every line.
5. Ask what is wrong or missing. Fix it. Save only after the owner says yes. Done when the owner approves the sheet.
6. Hand off. Tell the owner the next skill is `lead-magnet-idea-starter`, which finds a free item that leads into this offer. Offer to run it now.

## Regulated industries

If the business is in health, medical, mental health, wellness care, or finance:

- Describe the offer as education, support, or a service. Do not claim to cure, treat, fix, or guarantee any outcome.
- Do not promise results, savings, returns, or timelines.
- Where it fits, add an emergency line to the sheet, such as "If this is an emergency, call your local emergency number."
- Do not ask for health or money details in the interview beyond what the offer needs. Do not store them in the sheet.
- Ask the owner to have their own compliance contact review the sheet before it goes into an ad, quiz, or video.

## Pitfalls

- Owners list five offers. Pick one. A second offer can get its own sheet later.
- "Results" can drift into promises. Keep the result to what the owner can truly deliver, and write it as the goal, not a guarantee.
- A price the owner is unsure about is a [PLACEHOLDER], not a guess.
- Do not copy another company's offer, price, or proof into the sheet.

## Verification

The owner can read the one sentence offer out loud and say, "Yes, that is what I sell." Every proof or reason line is real or marked as a placeholder.

## Guardrails

- Read the owner's Company Brain first. If there is none, ask the owner to paste one or run `company-brain-starter` before you draft.
- Never invent facts, proof, prices, reviews, results, awards, locations, policies, or guarantees. If something is missing, write `[PLACEHOLDER]` and ask for it. Where proof is unsupported, write `[REAL PROOF NEEDED]`.
- Draft only. Do not send, publish, post, message leads, change records in any account, connect new accounts, or spend money without the owner's explicit approval of the exact action.
- Never ask the owner to paste a password, token, or API key into the chat. Point them to the provider's secure sign in screen or a private settings file on their own computer.
- Keep the writing plain, at a seventh grade reading level. Avoid hype words. Do not use em dashes or en dashes.
